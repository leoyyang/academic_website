My paper *"Scaling Reproducibility: An AI-Assisted Workflow for Large-Scale Replication and
Reanalysis"* has been **accepted for presentation** at the **First Annual Conference on AI
(Artificial Intelligence), Large Language Models, and the Social Sciences (LLMSS 2026)**, to be
held **14–16 October 2026 at City University of Hong Kong**. The paper appears in a thematic
panel session, with a dedicated discussant and time for questions and discussion; the detailed
panel programme and presentation slot will be circulated in September.

## Talk: Scaling Reproducibility

Computational reproducibility — the ability to regenerate a study's published numbers from its
own code and data — is central to scientific credibility, yet verifying results at scale remains
costly. The paper introduces an AI-assisted (artificial-intelligence-assisted) workflow for
automated full-paper replication: it retrieves replication materials, reconstructs the computing
environment, executes the original code, and matches the outputs to the point estimates reported
in published regression tables.

Key findings from the study:

- **Universe definition.** We define a universe of all empirical and quantitative papers from
  the three top political science journals (2010–2025) and measure stated data availability using
  automated extraction.
- **Replication at scale.** For a stratified sample of 384 studies, we apply the workflow to
  conduct full-paper replication, totaling 3,382 empirical models.
- **Impact of mandates.** Journal verification requirements, combined with data-archiving
  mandates, drive reproducibility: the full-paper reproducibility rate rises from 29.6% before
  DA-RT (Data Access and Research Transparency, a 2014 replication-transparency standard adopted
  across political science journals) adoption to 79.8% after, and — conditional on accessible
  replication packages — 94.4% of papers are fully reproducible (237 of 251).
- **Instrumental-variable diagnostics.** As a secondary application, we apply standardized
  instrumental-variable (IV, a method for estimating causal effects using an external source of
  variation) diagnostics to 92 studies (215 specifications), illustrating how automated execution
  enables systematic reanalysis across heterogeneous empirical settings.

## About the conference

LLMSS 2026 is organised by City University of Hong Kong's Computational Social Sciences Lab, the
University of Oxford's Nuffield College *Talking to Machines* Initiative, and Peking University's
Analytics Lab for Global Risk Politics, with support from the European Political Science
Association. Keynote speakers include **Issa Dahabreh** (Harvard University), **Scott Hale**
(Oxford Internet Institute), **Meng Tianguang** (Tsinghua University), and **Jonathan Zhu** (City
University of Hong Kong).

A day of pre-conference hands-on workshops (Tuesday, 13 October) covers topics such as
AI (Artificial Intelligence) public-opinion polling, digital twins, the Silicon Jury, and
prediction-powered inference. Accepted papers will be included in the open-access conference
proceedings.
