Invited by **Xi Weng** at the **Guanghua School of Management, Peking University**, I presented
at the "AI and Social Science" interdisciplinary seminar series.

## Talk: Scaling Reproducibility

Computational reproducibility is central to scientific credibility, yet verifying published
results at scale remains costly. I introduced an AI-assisted workflow for automated full-paper
replication — retrieving materials, reconstructing environments, executing code, and matching
outputs to point estimates reported in regression tables.

Key findings from the study:

- **Universe definition.** We define a universe of all empirical and quantitative papers from
  the three top political science journals (2010–2025) and measure stated data availability
  using automated extraction.
- **Replication at scale.** For a stratified sample of 384 studies, we apply the workflow to
  conduct full-paper replication, totaling 3,382 empirical models.
- **Impact of mandates.** Journal verification requirements, combined with data archiving
  mandates, drive reproducibility: the full-paper reproducibility rate rises from 29.6% before
  DA-RT adoption to 79.8% after, and conditional on accessible replication packages, 94.4%
  of papers are fully reproducible (237/251).
- **IV diagnostics.** As a secondary application, we apply standardized IV diagnostics to 92
  studies (215 specifications), illustrating how automated execution enables systematic
  reanalysis across heterogeneous empirical settings.
